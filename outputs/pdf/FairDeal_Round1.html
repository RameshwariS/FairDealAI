Round 1 abstract

FairDeal AI helps small stores negotiate prices and bundles with AI buyers while protecting margin, policy and truth.

The problem

AI buyers can exploit sellers that accept below cost, follow hidden instructions, trust fake budgets or deadlock.

What's new

AI handles conversation; deterministic code controls every money decision.

A hard policy gate

Every price clears per-item margin, discount and stock floors. Bundle totals keep each unit above its floor.

Offers move price, words don't

The engine responds to real bids and closes or walks by round 10. Text claims and hidden instructions never affect price.

Every claim checked

Offers cite store.csv and pass deterministic truth checks. TraceWare was tested against a mock; Labs access is pending.

Measured resistance

22 attacks gained nothing; 200 of 200 paired buyers with different words got identical prices. Ablation kept only useful rules.

Results

Scored by the kit's own evaluate.py (210 negotiations per run). Seeds 0–2 tuned our settings; seeds 3–6 never did.

Impact

Small sellers can serve AI shoppers with clearer terms. FairDeal understands Indian price phrases, including “15k” and “1.2 lakh”; its replay shows each decision.

Cost estimate

No model calls run on the core path: 0.11 ms per turn (p99 0.23 ms), 139 HTTP negotiations per second, and about $0.04 per million on an AWS t3.small in Mumbai.

Build summary

Built around a standard-library core: kit adapter, LLM buyers, red-team and ablation suites, and 40 pytest checks (38 pass, 2 expected failures).

AI reads and writes the conversation; code decides money. Every price, acceptance and walk-away comes from deterministic code, and a policy gate checks every message before it leaves the store.

Failure modeHow FairDeal AI blocks it
Below-cost or below-policy dealPer-unit floor = max(margin floor, list × (1 − max discount)), rounded up; the guard re-checks even the engine's output.
Accepting a bid with a slipped-in ₹1, out-of-stock or over-stock lineAn accept is sent only if every line of the buyer's exact bid passes policy; otherwise we offer their total split safely.
Fake budgets, competitor quotes, “manager approval”Logged and answered, never priced; prices written in buyer text are never read as bids.
Prompt injection (text, hidden fields, Unicode look-alikes, spoofed “SYSTEM”)Unicode is normalised and every field scanned; nothing from buyer text reaches pricing.
Fake “you already agreed to ₹X”A deal closes only at a price we actually offered.
DeadlockRound 9 offer at the buyer's projected limit; round 10 must accept, make a last offer the buyer can reach, or walk away.
Invented scarcity, urgency, fees; unsupported numbersBanned phrases, no prices for products not in play, and a number-by-number deterministic truth check.

Fairness and red-team

Price depends only on the basket, the round and the buyer's offers: 200 of 200 buyer pairs with identical offers but different words got identical prices. 22 of 22 named attacks defeated, each run beside an honest twin making the same offers (manipulation gain 0).

Testing and ablation

40 pytest checks run in CI: 38 pass and 2 F6 leak regressions are expected failures while that mitigation remains disabled. The suite includes a 3,000-message fuzz and the red-team suite. The kit's evaluate.py found two bugs our own tests had missed (a floating-point floor and stock booked for an accept we then blocked); both are fixed, with 0 violations across 1,470 official negotiations. The v2 ablation kept basket growth and the implied bid floor, while rejecting settings that reduced held-out performance.

TraceWare status

Tested against a mock; Labs access pending. The seller/traceware.py adapter is not validated against live Labs access, so no live TraceWare results are claimed.

Deployment, latency and cost

A web API (POST /negotiate) ships as a Docker image. The core path makes no model calls: 0.11 ms per turn (p99 0.23 ms), 139 negotiations per second over HTTP, about $0.04 of AWS t3.small time per million negotiations.
